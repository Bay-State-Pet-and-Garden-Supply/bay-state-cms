# Product-extraction health check: source research

**Research accessed:** 2026-10-21. This note records first-party/official sources and design implications; it does not establish whether any described capability exists or is absent in this repository.

## Evaluation and risk

- OpenAI's evaluation guidance describes reusable datasets containing inputs, outputs, annotations, and grader results; recommends broadening them with edge cases and blind spots, using human/subject-matter annotations as ground truth, and validating automated graders against human judgments. Its eval workflow includes defining objectives and metrics, comparing runs, and continuous evaluation. ([Evaluation getting started](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-getting-started); [evaluation best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** maintain a versioned representative gold set for page extraction, extend it with adjudicated production failures, score deterministic parsers and VLM/OCR against the same targets, and retain human review for ambiguous/subjective judgments; automated graders supplement, not replace, gold review.
- NIST AI RMF 1.0 names validity/reliability, safety, transparency, and management of harmful bias among trustworthiness characteristics; its framework organizes action as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), 2023; accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** treat extraction errors and unsafe/unsupported claims as measurable risks; document ownership, thresholds, escalation, and review rather than relying on aggregate accuracy alone.

## Product data and quality

- Google Search Central's product structured-data documentation distinguishes product snippets from merchant listing experiences and identifies product attributes such as name, image, price, availability, and product identifiers in its guidance. ([Product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** define field-level checks for identity and commercially consequential fields (e.g. product/variant identity, price, availability) against page evidence and applicable schema; schema compliance is not itself proof that extracted values are correct.
- OpenAI's documented grader types include exact/string checks, text similarity, model-based scoring/labels, and custom Python grading. ([Evaluation getting started](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-getting-started), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** exact-match normalized checks suit identifiers, prices, and enums; semantic or reviewed comparisons may suit descriptive text. Report precision and recall separately for populated/extracted fields, and measure abstention/coverage alongside correctness so conservative non-extraction is visible rather than counted as success.

## Segmentation, drift, and operations

- NIST AI RMF explicitly includes managing harmful bias and measurement of AI risks; OpenAI recommends datasets that expose edge cases and blind spots and continuous evaluation as cases emerge. ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf); [evaluation best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** stratify quality and abstention by extractor path (rules vs VLM/OCR), field, page/template family, input modality, and other relevant cohorts; inspect sample counts and uncertainty. Track quality over time and rerun a stable holdout plus newly adjudicated cases to detect regressions/drift; these are recommendations derived from the cited evaluation/risk guidance, not claims that a source prescribes a particular drift statistic.
- OpenTelemetry's metrics semantic conventions define common metric instruments and units, and general semantic conventions standardize attributes for telemetry. ([Metrics semantic conventions](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/general/metrics/); [general semantic conventions](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/general), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** record bounded-cardinality operational measures such as request counts, error/failure rates, and latency distributions by extraction path; preserve enough context to diagnose failure without labeling telemetry as quality evidence.
- OpenAI's evaluation guidance supports comparing runs and continuous evaluation, but the cited sources do not establish a universal extraction cost threshold or required latency SLO. ([Evaluation best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices), accessed 2026-10-21.) **Design implication:** include per-path latency, failure/timeout rate, and attributable compute/call cost where measurable, then set thresholds from this system's observed baseline and product requirements rather than borrowing unsupported universal limits.

## Scope boundary

This is external-source research only. Repository-specific existence or absence of datasets, graders, monitoring, structured-data validators, or operating thresholds is **not established by this research**.
